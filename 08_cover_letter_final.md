**Re: Application for Senior Lecturer (Grade DS13), Department of Software Engineering and Information Systems**

Dear Dean and Members of the Selection Committee,

I am writing to apply for the post of Senior Lecturer (DS13) in the Department of Software Engineering and Information Systems at FSKTM. I completed my PhD in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Universiti Malaysia Pahang Al-Sultan Abdullah (UMPSA) in August 2025, with a thesis on enhanced smoothed functional algorithms for data-driven modelling and control. My research area is metaheuristic computing, which is one of the five areas listed in your advertisement.

My publications from 2016 to 2025 include nine papers, three in Q1 journals (including IEEE Transactions on Industry Applications in 2025) and six as first author. My doctoral work proposed two variants of the smoothed functional algorithm, one with a norm-limited update vector and one with a comparative-memory scheme. I benchmarked them against particle swarm optimisation and simulated annealing, with improvements of up to 62.62% in the Function of Demerit. My other papers cover the marine predators algorithm, random search and the spiral dynamic algorithm. The MATLAB implementations of several of these algorithms are available at github.com/MokRH.

The other four areas in the advertisement are software testing, software maintenance, software security and AI for software engineering. I want to be direct that my published work is in optimisation and control, not yet in software engineering. What I bring is a method set that fits the field. My algorithms solve black-box, derivative-free problems with noisy objectives, which is the problem class addressed by search-based software engineering. At FSKTM, I plan to begin with test case generation and prioritisation, benchmarked against genetic algorithm, particle swarm and simulated annealing baselines on public benchmarks. I would then extend the work to search-based refactoring for maintainability, and to optimiser-tuned machine learning for defect and vulnerability prediction, and I would supervise postgraduate students on these topics.

In teaching and outreach, I was appointed in 2025 as a technical service provider for UMPSA's Innovation and Community Knowledge Transfer Grant, led by Assoc. Prof. Ir. Dr. Nurul Hazlina Binti Noordin, where I designed robot-based unplugged-coding activities, prepared a handling guide for teachers and gave them a brief training and demonstration. I have also contributed to Pahang's robotics co-curriculum programme for Orang Asli schools, including a workshop in October 2024, served as a trainer and judge for district robotics challenges in Pekan, and taught a robotics course at Kolej Komuniti Pekan in December 2023. I hold certificates or appointment letters for each. I am comfortable teaching programming in Python, C/C++ and MATLAB, and I would be glad to teach software testing, software maintenance, secure software development, artificial intelligence and optimisation. My industry experience at INCUS Solutions and RS Technologies in embedded software and hardware development gives me practical examples on testing, iteration and user feedback. I recognise that my formal lecturing experience is shorter than my research record, and I would welcome UPM's induction and mentoring as I develop it.

I completed my PhD while working: remotely for INCUS during the COVID-19 lockdown, when university laboratories were closed, as a graduate research assistant at UMP in 2021, and then part-time at RS Technologies. I delivered the INCUS project and my PhD on time. My current part-time, project-based role at RS Technologies carries no fixed contract, so I can take up the post immediately.
